# Fandom Harbor Figma Design Review

Status: Completed for UX-05 Homepage Visual Exploration; awaiting Product Owner acceptance
Phase: UX Design Intelligence
Scope: Figma exploration analysis only; not final UI, implementation, CSS, Tailwind, component design or code

## 1. Purpose

本文分析 Product Owner 提供的 Homepage Figma Exploration 素材，并将其转化为 Fandom Harbor 的 Homepage Visual Direction 判断依据。

本文件不复制任何单一参考方案，也不把参考页面视为最终设计稿。UX-05 只提取可迁移的 layout logic、experience pattern 和 visual principle，并结合 UX-01 至 UX-04 已验收基础重新判断。

## 2. Foundation Reference

UX-05 继承以下已验收基础：

- UX-01: Fandom Harbor 是安静、可信、文学、私域、可治理的作品归档与阅读产品。
- UX-02: Homepage 属于 Entry Space，负责解释私域归档和按访问状态分流。
- UX-03: 设计语言应是 Quiet Editorial、Private Archive、Precise Metadata、Soft Authority。
- UX-04: Homepage 是品牌入口，不是作品列表、营销页、论坛首页或公开社区页。

Why: Figma exploration 必须服务 Fandom Harbor 的品牌与 IA，而不是选择“最好看”的外部风格。
Impact: 后续 UX-06 可以判断视觉实现是否保持阅读产品方向。
Application: Homepage visual QA, UX-06 polish review, future Figma screen decisions.

## 5. Visual Analysis

### 5.1 Reference 1 — Editorial Whitespace and Brand Feeling

Useful principles:

- Strong editorial typography gives the page literary authority.
- Generous whitespace creates calm and premium restraint.
- Structured content groups feel curated rather than feed-driven.
- Table-of-contents logic could inspire archive orientation.

Risks:

- The issue / subscription model feels like a publication business rather than a private archive.
- Contributor portrait blocks can drift toward magazine masthead or social author showcase.
- The page includes subscription conversion patterns that do not fit the current Homepage purpose.

Why: This reference best supports Quiet Editorial and Premium Editorial Experience.
Impact: It can help Homepage feel branded without becoming decorative.
Application: Use its whitespace, typography hierarchy and editorial grouping logic; do not copy issue structure or subscription framing.

### 5.2 Reference 2 — Extreme Minimal Literary Index

Useful principles:

- Narrow text measure and restraint support long-form reading emotion.
- The page feels quiet, serious and non-commercial.
- Large empty space creates a contemplative pace.

Risks:

- Homepage becomes too much like an article index.
- It lacks access-state clarity, return path and author path.
- Discovery and next action are too understated for Fandom Harbor V1.

Why: It aligns with reading calm but under-serves Homepage as Entry Space.
Impact: Users may admire the mood but not know what to do next.
Application: Retain calm text rhythm as tone reference only; reject the full page structure.

### 5.3 Reference 3 — Work Presentation

Useful principles:

- Work titles, summaries and small metadata create a literary work-entry feeling.
- Text-led presentation avoids cover grid, ranking and social feed energy.
- Each work feels like a story invitation rather than a commodity card.

Risks:

- Metadata is too minimal for Fandom Harbor's rating, warning and tag needs.
- The repeated vertical rhythm can become too slow for discovery.
- Homepage could drift into a work list if this pattern dominates the first screen.

Why: This is the strongest reference for how Fandom Harbor can present works without becoming a generic novel grid.
Impact: Readers can sense story quality and archive care before entering Archive.
Application: Use for selected / featured story cues or homepage discovery preview, not as the entire Homepage.

### 5.4 Reference 4 — Navigation Structure

Useful principles:

- Sparse navigation supports intent-based movement.
- Top-level labels are few and calm.
- Strong first-viewport brand moment creates immediate identity.

Risks:

- Product-commerce hero and shop logic do not fit Fandom Harbor.
- Large lifestyle image could overpower text and reading purpose.
- Dark split hero may feel too luxury-retail or object-brand oriented.

Why: Navigation restraint is useful; the product-brand visual category is not.
Impact: Future Homepage can feel simpler and easier to enter without inheriting commerce mood.
Application: Translate navigation principle only: few entry points, clear access/action, no technical module names.

### 5.5 Reference 5 — Bottom Ending

Useful principles:

- The closing area feels calm, sparse and resolved.
- Footer navigation is restrained and not promotional.
- The end state gives the page a quiet landing rather than a hard conversion push.

Risks:

- Newsletter subscription is not the right primary relationship for Fandom Harbor.
- Retail navigation terms are not applicable.
- The ending must become archive/return oriented rather than inbox-oriented.

Why: The ending rhythm supports warm restraint and private archive tone.
Impact: Homepage can close with trust and orientation instead of marketing pressure.
Application: Use as inspiration for footer/end-of-page calm, sign-in/return guidance and archive identity.

## 6. Selected Direction

Recommended selected direction:

**Quiet Editorial Harbor**

This direction combines:

- Reference 1: whitespace, editorial authority and brand feeling.
- Reference 3: text-led work presentation and story invitation.
- Reference 4: sparse intent-based navigation.
- Reference 5: quiet closing rhythm.
- Reference 2: reading calm as tonal restraint, not structural model.

Core visual principle:

Homepage should feel like entering a protected literary archive: a calm editorial threshold that explains the harbor, offers a small number of meaningful paths, and gives a restrained preview of reading value without becoming a work list.

Why: This direction satisfies UX-01's private literary harbor, UX-02's Entry Space model, UX-03's Quiet Editorial design language and UX-04's Homepage purpose.
Impact: Users feel oriented, invited and ready to either continue reading, discover works or enter Studio if authorized.
Application: UX-06 Homepage implementation guidance, visual QA and navigation polish.

## 7. Rejected Direction

Rejected visual directions:

| Direction                        | Reason                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full magazine issue homepage     | Too publication/subscription-oriented; risks replacing private archive with editorial magazine model |
| Ultra-narrow text-only index     | Too passive for Entry Space; weak next action and access-state clarity                               |
| Product-commerce hero            | Too retail/luxury-object oriented; image can overpower story and archive purpose                     |
| Generic work grid or public list | Conflicts with UX-04: Homepage is not a work list                                                    |
| Trending / ranking homepage      | Violates No Popularity Dominance and Privacy Before Virality                                         |
| SaaS feature landing             | Conflicts with private archive and literary brand                                                    |
| CMS/dashboard entry              | Conflicts with Reader First and Studio-as-workroom principles                                        |

## 8. Design Reasoning

### Decision 1: Use editorial whitespace as brand structure

Why: Fandom Harbor's premium feeling should come from rhythm, hierarchy and calm space.
Impact: Homepage feels literary and trustworthy without decorative spectacle.
Application: UX-06 should preserve breathing room around brand threshold, access cues and featured story moments.

### Decision 2: Use text-led story presentation, not cover/feed presentation

Why: Works are literary archive objects; titles, summaries, warnings and metadata matter more than visual popularity.
Impact: Readers judge fit and tone without being pushed by images or social heat.
Application: UX-06 should keep story preview secondary to Homepage purpose and avoid turning Home into Archive.

### Decision 3: Keep navigation sparse and intent-based

Why: UX-02 defines navigation around enter, discover, read, return and create.
Impact: Users understand where to go without learning technical sections.
Application: UX-06 should review navigation labels for intent clarity and avoid CMS-like menu density.

### Decision 4: Use a quiet closing, not a conversion ending

Why: Fandom Harbor is invitation and trust driven, not subscription conversion driven.
Impact: Page ending reinforces private archive identity and recovery paths.
Application: UX-06 should replace newsletter/shop patterns with sign-in, archive, continue or identity-oriented closure.

## 9. UX-06 Implementation Guidance

UX-06 should use this review as visual QA criteria:

- Homepage must not become a generic novel grid, public ranking page or SaaS landing page.
- Hero / threshold area must explain private archive value and next action.
- Typography should carry literary brand and remain readable with mixed-language content.
- Work preview patterns should include enough metadata direction for reader trust.
- Navigation should be sparse, permission-aware and intent-based.
- Footer/ending should feel calm and archive-oriented.
- Any implementation should use existing codebase patterns and separately authorized token/component decisions.
